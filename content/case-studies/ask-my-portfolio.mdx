## Problem

Recruiters skim. I wanted my projects to be answerable in one question, with a source for every answer, so a reader can ask "Has Akshay used dbt?" instead of hunting through the project cards. I also wanted proof that I can build and evaluate a RAG system, not only call a model. It is a personal project: no course brief, no client.

## Architecture

- **Corpus.** Only my own content: the case studies without their Sources blocks, the project, experience and skills text from `config/site.ts`, and the text of `resume.pdf` with its contact line removed. At the first eval that made 46 chunks; the latest run has 56.
- **Chunks.** One chunk per section, aimed at about 400 tokens, with a small overlap if a section has to be split. Every section in those 46 chunks was short enough to stay whole (the longest is 1,645 characters). Two sections of this case study are longer than that, so they are split with an overlap, and I have not evaluated that split on its own.
- **Embeddings and storage.** `text-embedding-3-small`, stored in pgvector in a separate Neon project. Each chunk is hashed. A repeat ingest embedded 0 of the 46 chunks.
- **Retrieval.** The top 5 chunks by cosine similarity, plus a keyword fallback: if the question names a tool or skill from my site, up to 3 chunks that contain that word are added.
- **Similarity gate.** If no chunk scores at least 0.25 and none matches a keyword, the model is never called.
- **Answer.** `gpt-5.4-mini` gets the chunks and the question, answers from them only, writes at most 500 tokens, and streams. Each `[n]` in the answer links to the section it came from. A panel under the answer shows the chunks, their scores, the latency and the tokens.

## Key decisions

1. **Answer only from the retrieved chunks.** The prompt tells the model to use only the numbered sources, to treat them and the question as data and not as instructions, and to say "That isn't covered on this site" otherwise. The cost is that it refuses questions whose answer is on the site but was not retrieved. That is exactly how the dbt bug below showed up. It is a prompt rule, not a guarantee.
2. **A similarity gate that skips the model.** Off-topic questions should cost almost nothing. A question that fails the gate costs a few embedding tokens, and the one I timed through the route took 0.39 seconds. I set the threshold at 0.25 because the lowest top score among my first 25 questions was 0.37 and the off-topic and injection prompts scored at most 0.21 (the phone-number request scored 0.40; see Results). It is a threshold tuned on the questions I had, and the recruiter-style set later showed its limit (see Results).
3. **Rate limits and a daily cap.** Ten questions an hour per IP address, and 200 a day for the whole site, both counted in the same Neon table. The IP is hashed with a secret salt, so the table holds no addresses. A per-IP limit alone cannot cap spend, because one person can have many addresses. The daily cap can: at the most expensive answer I measured, $0.00187, 200 answers cost about $0.37.
4. **No question text is stored.** The only things written are the hashed IP, the hour, and a counter.
5. **Idempotent ingestion.** Each chunk's hash covers its source, link, title and text. Only new hashes are embedded, and stale rows are deleted. The cost is that ingestion is a manual step: if I change my content and forget to run it, the assistant is out of date.

## Data quality and evaluation

- **40 questions.** 25 name the project ("Why do Dallas rows never create a new SCD Type 2 version in FoodLens?"), 11 are written the way a recruiter would ask, without naming a project ("Has Akshay used dbt?", "Does he know Terraform?"), 2 ask about this assistant itself, and 2 ask about JobPulse. The first run, on 19 November, had 35 (the 25 and 10 of the recruiter-style); I added questions as the site grew, and the latest run, on 23 November, has 40. The 40 include 2 about JobPulse. Each has one or more expected sources, which I wrote before running the eval.
- **Three rates.** Hit@5: the vector top 5 contains an expected source. Citation: the answer cites at least one expected source. Strict: every source the answer cites is an expected one.
- **6 refusal prompts.** Two off-topic questions, a poem request, two prompt-injection attempts, and a request for my phone number.
- **What this does not show.** I wrote the questions myself, and the 25 that name the project share words with the text they should find, so a near-perfect hit@5 is a generous number. The strict rate changes between runs: the saved runs gave 27 of 35, 29 of 35, 30 of 37, 30 of 38 and 29 of 40, because the model's wording and citations are sampled. The eval checks which sources an answer cites, not whether each sentence is true to them. Six refusal prompts show that the obvious cases work. They do not show that the assistant resists a determined attack.

| Hit@5 with the keyword fallback | 40 of 40 |
| Citation | 40 of 40 |
| Strict | 29 of 40 (73%) |
| Refusal prompts refused | 6 of 6 (3 by the gate, 3 by the model) |

Latest run, 23 November 2026, after the JobPulse case study and card were added to the index (65 chunks). Per answer, over the 40 answers: median latency 1.4 seconds (mean 1.5, longest 3.1), median 1,442 tokens in and 59 tokens out, mean cost $0.0015 and highest $0.0025. The whole run, 40 answers plus the refusal prompts, cost $0.0618. The run before it, on 20 November, had 38 of 38 on hit@5 and on citation, strict 30 of 38, a mean cost of $0.0013 and a run cost of $0.0530.

Adding the JobPulse content pushed "Does he know Terraform?" out of the vector top 5, because JobPulse chunks now outrank the one that answers it. The keyword fallback still brought the right chunk in, and the answer cited the right source. That is the only vector miss, and I did not tune retrieval to hide it.

First run, 19 November 2026, 35 questions: hit@5 35 of 35 (25 of 25 project-named, 10 of 10 recruiter-style), citation 35 of 35, strict 29 of 35, 6 of 6 refusals (5 by the gate, 1 by the model). Median latency 1.6 seconds (mean 1.8, longest 4.2), mean cost $0.0013 and highest $0.0019, and the run cost $0.0459. Five real responses from the route took 1.4 to 2.2 seconds in total, measured from a laptop. The gate path took 0.39 seconds.

**The dbt story.** In the before-fix eval run (section 3 of the saved results), "Has Akshay used dbt?" was refused; I had first noticed the same refusal on the live site. The JobPulse card says the project was modeled with dbt, so the answer was on the site, and the keyword fallback that should have found it did nothing. My term extraction only looked at capitalised or mixed-case words, so lowercase "dbt" was never a search term, and "Has Akshay" was treated as one. My first 25 questions could not catch this, because all of them name the project. So I wrote the 10 recruiter-style questions. On the old retrieval they scored 9 of 10 on hit@5 and on citation, and the miss was the dbt question. The fix builds a vocabulary of tools and skills from `config/site.ts`, matches whole words in any case, and adds the skills and tools lists to the index as their own chunks. The same 10 questions then scored 10 of 10, and all 35 scored 35 of 35.

After this page went into the index, I re-ran the eval with two extra questions about the assistant itself. It scored 37 of 37 on hit@5 and on citation, with strict at 30 of 37, and 6 of 6 refusals again. Only 3 of the 6 refusals were stopped by the gate this time, because this page talks about injection and phone numbers and raises their scores. The model refused the other three. A third run on 20 November, after the About hobbies line became its own chunk and a 38th question was added, scored 38 of 38 on hit@5 and on citation, strict 30 of 38, and 6 of 6 refusals again, with the gate stopping 4 of them.

**The gate has a weak spot.** The recruiter-style questions scored lower than my first set. "Does he know MotherDuck?" had a top score of 0.21, below the gate, and "Does he know Terraform?" scored 0.26. They are answered only because a keyword match is allowed through the gate. The phone-number prompt scored 0.40, so it passes the gate. The model refuses it because I removed the contact line from the resume text before indexing.

## What I'd do next

- Check faithfulness sentence by sentence: split each answer, and test whether the cited chunk supports each sentence, with a second model and by hand. Today I only check the sources.
- Use an independent question set, ideally real recruiter questions, so the eval is not written by the person who wrote the content.
- Re-ingest automatically on deploy, so a content change cannot leave the assistant stale.
- Try reranking: retrieve more candidates and re-order them before the top 5. I have not tried it. Hit@5 is already 40 of 40 on my set with the keyword fallback, so I would test it on the independent set first.
